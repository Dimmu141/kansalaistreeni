# First learner pilot — prepared, not conducted

Use this to test whether the app helps someone learn and understand what to do next. It is not an exam-readiness validation or a statistically representative study. No participants have been recruited or sessions run.

Start with 3–5 adult learners with different Finnish/Swedish reading levels and Android experience. Use a test device and fresh demo history. Explain that questions, topics and mastery are provisional and are not official exam coverage. Ask for permission before taking notes or recording; participation can stop at any point. Do not collect immigration status, application details or unnecessary identifying information. Use participant codes in notes.

## A 25-minute session

1. Open Home without explanation. Ask: “What is this app for? What does this percentage tell you? What would you do next?” Record their interpretation before correcting it.
2. Ask them to choose their preferred study language and open a topic. Let them discover the reading lesson and explanations. Ask them to explain one idea in their own words, then start the topic questions.
3. Let them answer and use feedback. Observe whether they find the native explanation, easier Finnish/English support and source, and whether feedback changes their understanding. Avoid guiding them to the correct answer.
4. Ask them to find a civic word, limit vocabulary to a topic, inspect its context and try word practice. Ask what they think word practice does to their mastery score.
5. Ask them to return Home, inspect Progress/Review and explain their next step. Note whether “not due yet” is understood and whether a first correct guess feels like evidence of mastery.
6. On the test device, ask them to export a backup, open its preview and explain what restoring would replace. Cancel it first. If useful, complete a disposable test restore together; do not overwrite anyone's real history.

Use Finnish or Swedish study content with whichever interface language they understand. Offer large text and the learner's usual accessibility settings. Record observed navigation problems separately from reading-level problems; do not assume every difficulty needs more interface text.

## Notes and decisions

For each participant code, record study/interface language, voluntary broad self-described reading comfort, task outcome (independent / needed help / could not finish), misunderstood wording, where they got stuck, their interpretation of mastery and unofficial status, and one thing they learned. Do not invent outcomes, percentages or quotes. Record quotes only with permission.

Treat repeated confusion as a reason to investigate, not a measured population rate. Before a wider pilot, resolve any belief that the score predicts passing, that sources make demo questions official, that reading creates mastery, or that restore merges rather than replaces history. Fix blockers in completing a lesson, finding feedback or returning to the next learning action.

After the sessions, choose a small number of evidence-based changes. Reconsider whether topic reading should always precede practice, whether word recall needs its own saved review model, and whether the mastery heuristic communicates too much confidence. Leave exam format, pass predictions and mock-exam thresholds undefined until official material and suitable evidence exist.
